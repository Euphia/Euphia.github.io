## Speaking in Neutral
Some people speak in Neutral.Some speak in fuller & reduced density Neutral some speak in Neutral in falsetto.The character of the voice for all 3 Neutrals are gentle, non-breathy or breathy, light or dark, but the volume is not particularly loud.Speakers using mainly Neutral usually have many possibilities for varying their speech and have a large range with many tonal variations.However, such people normally have problems when they try to speak loudly.The Neutral mode is not suited to loud speaking or singing except for in the high part, and the voice can break, split or get strained if you try.People who speak in Neutral often find their voices tire quickly and feel that it is difficult to be heard.People speaking mainly in Neutral also often choose a higher pitch when trying to speak loudly.

### Speaking in the different densities of Neutral
Neutral in falsetto can be used for speaking voice.However, when speaking in Neutral in falsetto, the pitch is often higher and the sound of the voice can be a little thin or hollow, due to the character of the falsetto sound.Speakers using Neutral in falsetto are often female and rarely male speakers.Speakers choosing to use Neutral in falsetto often find it difficult speaking in lower pitches and can find it difficult being heard as louder volume is not easy in the Neutral mode when speaking.Many speakers who use mainly Neutral in falsetto experience vocal breaks as they try to be louder, because turning up the volume they often end in reduced or fuller Neutral or a metallic mode.

Neutral in falsetto is probably the most common used of the various Neutrals.Almost all singers know this thin and hollow version of Neutral.It is usually also the easiest to control as it generally doesn’t cost much support energy.This is why Neutral in Falsetto often is chosen where fuller and reduced density in Neutral are difficult to maintain and cost a lot in support energy.The change to Neutral in falsetto is often done unconsciously as the voice tends to seek the easiest solution.This change can be done abruptly or inaudibly.Both the abruptly and inaudibly transition can be trained so the you are in charge of your choices and can achieve the sound you prefer.

The setting, control and sound in Neutral in falsetto is often regarded as quite different to the setting, control and sound in fuller and reduced density Neutral.

Neutral with reduced density can also be used for speaking voice.When speaking with reduced density Neutral, the character becomes contained or restrained, and the pitch is also often a little higher, as reduced density is difficult to obtain in the low and lower part of the voice.Speakers using Neutral with reduced density may find it difficult to turn up the volume, and if they do, they often end up in Curbing or reduced Overdrive or Edge, meaning, they are limited to medium loud volumes, unless they train the transitions to either fuller density Neutral, Neutral in falsetto or metallic modes.

Neutral with fuller density can also be used for speaking voice.When choosing fuller density for Neutral, the voice sounds richer and fuller compared to reduced density and falsetto.Speakers using fuller density Neutral can use all ranges and parts of the voice, but will be limited to soft and medium volumes as Neutral is not suited for loud volumes.Speakers using Neutral with fuller density may find it difficult to turn up the volume, and if they do, they often end up in Overdrive or Edge if the speaker are familiar with the sound from the metallic modes.

It is important to be aware of which setting for Neutral you are using for speech to use the voice healthily, as the support values and characters differ between them.Neutral in falsetto, generally, will cost less support energy and speakers accustomed to speaking with a falsetto setting may not be aware of how much more support energy it takes to speak in reduced or fuller density Neutral.Reduced and fuller density Neutral will usually require more support energy but will usually also make smooth transitions into metallic modes easier, reducing the potential for pushing the volume limits of the Neutral mode.

Many female speakers choose, often unconsciously, to speak from a Neutral with a falsetto setting, either due to fashion, culture, or habit.In certain jobs requiring loud speaking, for instance being an actor, or an aerobics instructor the vocal demands for loudness can be high.Therefore, a large amount of female speakers run into the volume limits of the Neutral mode for speaking and can experience fatigue or tiredness and this group of speakers often have more troubles being heard in noisy environments.It is always recommendable to be able to dynamically change the voice in a conscious manner to avoid misusing the voice.Such a conscious manner involves knowing how to change from a falsetto setting to either reduced or fuller density Neutral or metallic modes.

### Exercises in speaking in fuller density Neutral
At first, practise the Neutral in fuller density.



> See 'Finding fuller density in Neutral (highway neutral)’


> Speaking in fuller density Neutral


> ‘Speaking’ example


You can start finding fuller density Neutral by singing a note in Overdrive or Edge in a comfortable pitch on the vowel EH (as in stay) in volume 8.Notice the clear metallic and often direct, bright, unrestrained, and ‘shouty’ character of Overdrive, like when you call ‘hey’ after someone in the street.Now keep this clear metallic and open sensation, while gradually decreasing the volume to 7, then 6, then 5.Be aware that you still keep the clear and open sensation and then decrease the volume to 4, while changing the vowels sound slightly to EE (as in see).Then continue decreasing the volume to volume 3, and 2 and maintain the clear and open sensation.Around Volume 3-4 you should be in fuller density Neutral.If you maintain the clear and open sensation, you can practise speaking as quiet as possible without losing the sensation.If all of a sudden you feel that the sensation changes, turn up the volume a little and return to the fuller density Neutral sound where you had the clear and open sensation.

Now gradually practise maintaining that sound in various pitches from volume 4 to 1.

Start in a comfortable pitch and repeat the exercise, beginning half a note higher each time.

To practice the setting into a lower pitch for speaking, start in a comfortable pitch and repeat the exercise, beginning half a note lower each time.When you approach the pitch for speaking, try to make a transition from singing to speaking by making the sung note shorter and shorter until you are actually speaking.Make sure that speaking in fuller density Neutral, just like speaking in the other modes, always feels comfortable.

Now try to practise all the other vowels.Make sure you maintain the fuller density Neutral character and the quiet volume, (from 1-4, out of 10)

Notice the sensation and character when speaking in fuller density Neutral.Also notice how close you are to the border of fuller density Overdrive.Practise making the transition from fuller density Neutral to fuller density Overdrive, so you at all times can control and know in which mode and density you speak.

### Exercises in speaking in reduced density Neutral
At first, practise the Neutral in reduced density.



> See 'Finding reduced density in Neutral (byway neutral)’


> Speaking in reduced density Neutral


> ‘Speaking’ example


If you want to transfer this sound to speech, it must be practised.You can start singing a note in Curbing in a comfortable pitch on the vowel ‘UH’ (as in ‘hungry’) in volume 6, Notice the light sensation of the ‘hold’.。Be aware that you still keep the sensation of the slight ‘hold’, then decrease the volume to volume 3, and 2 but maintain the sensation of the slight ‘hold’.Around volume 3-4 you should be in reduced density Neutral.If you maintain the sensation of the slight ‘hold’, you can practise singing as quietly as possible without losing the sensation and thereby setting.If all of a sudden you feel that the sensation changes, turn up the volume a little and return to the reduced density Neutral sound where you had the sensation of the slight ‘hold’.

To exercise the setting into your speaking voice, start in a comfortable pitch and repeat the exercise, beginning half a note lower each time.When you approach the pitch for speaking, try to make a transition from singing to speaking by making the sung note shorter and shorter until you are actually speaking.Make sure that speaking in reduced density Neutral, just like speaking in the other modes, always feels comfortable.

Now try to practise other all the other vowels.Make sure you maintain the reduced density Neutral character and the quiet volume, from 1-4 out of 10.

Notice the sensation and character when speaking in reduced density Neutral.Also notice how close you are to the border of Curbing.Practise making the transition from reduced density Neutral to Curbing, so you at all times can control and know in which mode and density you speak and hereby have a path to obtain more volume and to avoid becoming restricted volumewise.

### Exercises in speaking in Neutral in falsetto
At first, practise the Neutral in falsetto.



> See 'Finding Neutral in falsetto’


> Speaking in Neutral in falsetto


> ‘Speaking’ example


If you want to transfer this sound to speech, it must be practiced.Sing with a lot of vocal breaks, notice the tiny sound that you are breaking to and from.This tiny sound is Neutral with falsetto.Now sustain these notes and notice the sensation when singing in Neutral in falsetto.Practise singing higher and lower in pitch with Neutral in falsetto.Notice that Neutral with falsetto often works better in the higher part of the voice and the volume can be altered from quiet to loud in the higher part.Also notice that Neutral with falsetto gets increasingly hard to control, the lower the pitch.

Therefore, start in a comfortable pitch and repeat the exercise, beginning half a note lower each time.Find a pitch that could be suitable for speaking, even though it might be a little high pitched compared to where you usually are speaking.Try to make a transition from singing to speaking in this pitch by making the sung note shorter and shorter until you are actually speaking.Make sure that speaking in Neutral in falsetto, just like speaking in the other modes, always feels comfortable.

Now try to practise other all the other vowels in Neutral in falsetto.Make sure you maintain the falsetto character and the quiet volume, from 1-4 out of 10.

Notice the sensation and character when speaking in Neutral in falsetto.Also notice how far you are from the other modes and densities.Usually breaks will occur between the disengaged singing (Neutral with falsetto and the engaged singing (all modes in fuller & reduced density).Practise making the transition from engaged to disengaged speaking in a smooth manner so you at all times can control and know whether you speak in fuller & reduced density Neutral or in Neutral in falsetto.Notice the increased amount of support energy you need for speaking in fuller & reduced density Neutral compared to speaking in Neutral in falsetto.

Take care that you are not only practicing Neutral in falsetto when practising speaking in Neutral; remember also to practice fuller and reduced density.Speaking in Neutral in falsetto usually costs less in support energy than fuller and reduced density Neutral and therefore often lures you into forgetting the support values from the ‘normal’ engaged speaking (speaking in Neutral in fuller & reduced density, Curbing, fuller & reduced density in Overdrive and Edge).If you persist speaking only disengaged (Neutral in falsetto) the result could be that you might end up only having the thin and hollow sound left.To avoid this practice until you can add and remove Neutral in falsetto at will in your speaking.
