## Trouble-Shooting

## Solving unintentional vocal breaks
A vocal break is an abrupt change in sound.Vocal breaks can be a means of expression and are used in many styles of singing.



> See 'Intentional Vocal Breaks'


This chapter will cover unintentional vocal breaks, which prevent evenness in singing.

The abrupt change of sound occurs between any mode in fuller or reduced density - to Neutral in falsetto.Therefore, a Vocal Break will always involve a change to or from Neutral in falsetto



> See ‘Neutral in falsetto’


This often happens involuntarily because the vowel, the volume, or the pitch might ‘lure’ you to change the mode or density to Neutral in falsetto, often because there is insufficient support energy.

### Changing to Neutral in falsetto
Unwanted breaks or changes in the voice are usually a result of an unconscious change between disengaged singing (Neutral in falsetto) to engaged singing (fuller & reduced density Neutral, Curbing, or fuller & reduced density in Overdrive and Edge)

If a singer does not deliberately choose her/his setting, the voice will often choose it for her/him with the result being that the singing has a different sound to the one intended.Then the singer starts constricting uncontrolledly to avoid this, which can result in the voice becoming strained.

If the singer obeys the rules of the vocal modes and density instead, it is possible to achieve the sound s/he wishes, and unwanted breaks are avoided.

### An example of an unintentional break
Most singers have no difficulty getting power in the lower part of the voice, and many have cultivated a powerful, rich, open character in this part of the voice.This is Overdrive and it is often the preferred mode in the low part of the voice.When singing in the higher parts of the voice, the support requires more physical strength, especially if the singer wishes to continue singing in Overdrive.This may become difficult.If the voice chooses the Neutral in falsetto instead of Overdrive in the higher parts of the voice, the support only requires, let’s say, half the energy.This quickly becomes apparent to the body and thus Neutral in falsetto becomes the preferred mode in the high part of the voice.The change between the two modes and their densities is heard as a change of sound colour or as a ‘break’ that some mistakenly refer to as a change of register.In fact, it has nothing to do with a change of register but is a change of vocal mode and/or density.



> A break due to change to Neutral in falsetto

It is more appropriate for the singer to know when s/he is close to the limit of the capacity of the vocal mode, and therefore intentionally chooses to change the vocal mode or density into a more suitable one, instead of letting the voice choose, which might lead to an unintentional break.

Each vocal mode or its density has special characteristics, advantages and limitations with regard to volume, use of vowels, pitch and sound colour.It is important to choose the most suitable vocal mode or density for what you wish to sing, and to be able to avoid unintentional breaks.

### Misleading terminology
Vocal breaks have nothing to do with a change of register.This is illustrated by the fact that the voice of a given singer may break on many different notes.Vocal breaks can occur at any pitch when we change between the engaged and disengaged singing (Neutral in falsetto and any other mode and density).It is not logical to name these breaks as a ‘change of register’.A register is a certain part of the voice, pitch-wise, which has a fixed position and does not move from note to note.Changing register means changing between parts of the voice in pitch, and a vocal break is not necessarily related to a pitch change between different parts of the voice.These different pitch parts of the voice have no one characteristic sound colour or volume, so it is misleading and often harmful to talk about ‘pulling up the chest voice’ when you want a powerful volume and resonant sound on high notes.

To talk about ‘a chest voice sound’ and ‘a head voice sound’ is often misleading.Notes in ‘chest voice´ or ‘head voice’ can sound in many different ways depending on the vocal mode used.At best, these expressions are illogical; at worst, they cause considerable vocal problems by luring a singer into believing that s/he can maintain a given sound by maintaining the vibration (buzzing) in the chest on high notes.This often introduces uncontrolled constriction, which prevents you from reaching high notes, and is liable to strain the voice.It can be quite a task to get rid of such uncontrolled constriction.

So, you must remember the three overall principles and respect the rules of the vocal modes.For example, practise singing scales up and down in each vocal mode in order to avoid changes and breaks.Later you can practise making smooth transitions between the modes and their densities to Neutral in falsetto to avoid unintentional vocal breaks.



> See ‘Transitions between modes’


### Failing to support
Another frequent reason for an unintentional change of vocal mode or density and the consequent break or change of voice is insufficient support energy.As before, a vocal break might occur on different notes, depending on where the singer loses her/his support energy and thereby changes to Neutral in falsetto.If you start by singing with so much power that you do not have sufficient support energy to continue the volume it is called ‘failing to support’.



> A break due to change of mode


### Volume 5 and volume 10
Higher notes require higher support energy.A higher volume also requires a higher support value.An example could be, the support values for pitch and volume can be multiplied.If a singer has to sing an ascending scale at volume 5, the support values for each individual note will be, for example: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 and so on.If the singer instead chooses volume 10, the support values could be 5 times higher: 5, 10, 20, 40, 80 and so on.




In the lower part of the middle of the voice, where you are speaking, support values might not be so high and multiplying these low values by 5 seldom presents a problem.When you sing in the higher part of the voice (or the very low part of the voice), the support values quickly become enormously high when multiplied by 5, and it becomes difficult to apply enough support energy.Somewhere on this scale a singer might suddenly run out of support energy and the voice will change to Neutral in falsetto that requires less support energy in that area of the voice.As a result, the voice may break from engaged singing to disengaged singing and the volume changes abruptly, for example from volume 10 to volume 2.

These breaks are also often mistaken for ‘changes of register’ that are hard to avoid.As before, this is a change to or from Neutral in falsetto and any vocal mode and density, which can easily be avoided by controlling the vocal modes and densities and Neutral in falsetto, and the transitions between them.

A failure to support is heard more clearly in the higher part of the voice, for example, in the high part of a man’s voice.If there is insufficient support energy for a given note, the character of the note changes to a thin, squeaking sound (Neutral in falsetto).So, you have to be careful to maintain the character up through the high part of the voice if you want a consistent sound.To maintain the character in the high part of the voice requires, as in other parts of the voice, more control.

If it is impossible to avoid a break or a change in sound, perhaps you do not have the necessary physical strength the support value, vocal mode or density demands in order to reach the pitch in the specific mode and density.Therefore, if you do not have the physical strength to complete a song in volume 9, lower the volume and maybe change the vocal mode or density until you can manage it.



> See ‘Transitions between modes’


### Support energy is necessary
Learn how to economise your strength.Start, for example, at volume 1 and when you are able to complete the song with a sound you like, increase the volume to 2, thereby making the exercise harder and physically more demanding.Then continue increasing the volume to 3, 4 and so on.Remember that you have to change mode according to the volume.In general, in the middle part of the voice, Neutral in fuller & reduced density has to be sung within volumes 1-4, Curbing within volumes 4-7, and Overdrive and Edge within volumes 4-10.Never sing louder that you have enough support energy for, which means that the song can be completed without changing volume or sound.Train your strength and stamina.Train the support muscles so you build up the necessary strength.If there were two identical singers, the physically stronger one would be able to sing higher and more powerfully than the less strong one.


## Exercises for avoiding unwanted vocal breaks
There are two ways of avoiding unwanted breaks.One is to avoid Neutral in falsetto, and instead use any other mode in fuller or reduced density.The other is to learn how to make smooth inaudible transitions between any mode and density - to Neutral in falsetto.

### Avoid Neutral in falsetto
If the voice breaks, start by practising the notes around the break separately and notice which modes and density you are singing in.If you want to avoid the break avoid using Neutral in falsetto.This often means that you will have to use more support energy in order not to change to Neutral in falsetto.Slowly practise the notes around the break and memorise the necessary support values to avoid Neutral in falsetto.When you are able to sing each note without changing to Neutral in falsetto (which means without breaking), put the notes together into small melodic sequences.Use the memorised support values and gradually make the exercises more difficult until you have solved the problem of the unintentional break.

### Inaudible transition to and from falsetto
The other way to avoid a vocal break into or from falsetto, is to train smooth and inaudible transitions from any vocal mode and density to Neutral in falsetto.Remember, transitions to and from Neutral in falsetto can be difficult to perform without a break.These transitions are usually technical demanding, so they often need to be trained separately.

To make inaudible transitions to or from Neutral in falsetto, it can be a great help to use the hold from the reduced density when changing to or from Neutral in falsetto.With this setting it should be possible to make an inaudible transition to and from falsetto.Once you have made a transition from Neutral in falsetto to reduced density you can change to any mode in fuller or reduced density.



> See ‘Transition to and from falsetto’


In the beginning take your time and practise the inaudible transition to and from falsetto via the reduced density setting.When you control that, you can practise the change from Neutral in falsetto into any mode or density you want.Practise until the transition becomes smooth and inaudible.Also practise the opposite way from any mode or density you want to Neutral in falsetto with a smooth and inaudible transition.






> Avoiding breaks on ascending melodic lines


> See ‘Transitions between modes’





Avoid breaks on descending melody lines.The voice will often jump down into a heavier character (e.g. the Overdrive mode, with fuller density) if you do not control it by using more support energy.It requires a lot of support energy to sing in Neutral in reduced density and Curbing in the lower part of the voice.Be familiar with the areas of your voice that are prone to breaks and avoid Neutral in falsetto by giving more support energy in these places to stay in the chosen vocal mode or density.



> Avoiding breaks on descending melodic lines


Train the flexibility of your voice by practising healthy routines where you avoid breaking all the way through your vocal range.Practise at all volumes and with all pitches, sound colours, tempi and vowels until you are able to avoid breaks.



> Practise flexibility in connection with the various parts of the voice, sound colours, vowels, volume, and tempos


Remember: if the same exercise fails three times in a row, it is too difficult, and you will only work in uncontrolled constriction by continuing.Make the exercise easier in order to succeed and work these healthy routines into your muscle memory.
