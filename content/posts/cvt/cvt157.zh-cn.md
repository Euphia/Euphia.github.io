## 练习特殊转换 Exercising specific transitions
### 与音高相关的转换 Transitions of modes in relation to pitch
在练习模式之间的转换时，重要的是要严格注意音高，不要用过高的音高来唱，例如在使用 Overdrive 的时候。

你唱得越高，模式常常就越清晰。因此，音越高，就越难丝滑地转换模式。

最好可以做某首歌具体问题的针对性练习。例如，如果你通常以响亮的音量唱歌，那么就要做到能够从 Overdrive 切换到 Edge 而不出现破音或听感突变。

### 案例故事 Case Story
一位声音极具表现力的歌唱家、演员无法在高音区唱出响亮的声音，声音单薄、呜咽。这是很糟糕的，因为她的个性和戏剧天赋需要响亮的音量和较大的动态范围。她在低音区歌唱时没有问题，但在高音区会出现断音，从断音的地方开始，声音就变得单薄而虚弱。问题在于她会在中低音区使用 Overdrive，但只能用假声 Neutral 才能在高音区唱上去。不过由于她身强体壮，支承扎实有力，我们直接开始练习 Edge。She was already used to twanging the epiglottic funnel, as she had done this to avoid the sound becoming too operatic in the high part of the voice.So, all she needed was to add the fuller metallic sound with fuller density.

We worked on sustaining the energy required for Overdrive further up her voice, where she found Edge.By doing this, she could continue the same volume as in Overdrive into the highest part of her voice by changing to Edge.After about an hour’s work, she was able to Edge up to the High C. Having gained control over Overdrive and Edge, she soon became nothing short of an institution in many major musical theatre productions.


## Exercise in fuller density
– from Overdrive to Edge
To change from Overdrive to Edge, it is important to start the transition before getting too high in pitch.It works best for women between A4-C5 and for men between G4-Bb4.It is necessary to make the Overdrive gradually lighter by increasing the twang of the epiglottic funnel and by directing all vowels towards the twanged vowels.Remember, the closer you get to the upper limit of Overdrive, the more shouting the character and volume can become.




Sing single notes in Overdrive on the vowel ‘EH’ (as in ‘stay’).Start on G4 for women or C4 for men and sing in a fairly powerful volume without it feeling uncomfortable or strenuous.Listen to the shouty, fuller density and clear character and keep this sound while you ascend by half steps.From A4-C5 for women or from E4-G4 for men gradually twang the epiglottic funnel (it may also help to raise the larynx, relax the palate and broaden the tongue) without changing the sound between the notes.At around C5/D5 for women or G4 for men you should be in Edge and you can continue singing higher from here.






> Exercise from Overdrive to Edge


If you find it difficult to move from Overdrive to Edge without an abrupt change, try the above exercise starting from the high notes.Sing single notes in Edge on D5 for women or G4 for men and descend by semitones, while trying to make the sound colour of each note less twanged, but still powerful.Be careful not to darken the sound colour TOO much as it can strain the voice.By doing this carefully and correctly, you should be in Overdrive at about A4 for women or E4 for men.

Once you can control this transition, sing a longer sequence, such as scales up and down through three notes, five notes and later, through octaves.Be careful not to sing in too high a pitch for Overdrive.At first practise on ‘EH’ as this vowel relates to both Overdrive and Edge.Then start practising in Overdrive on the vowel ‘OH’ (as in ‘so’) and changing it to ‘OE’ (as in ‘herb’) in the higher part of the voice as you change to Edge.Then practise the transition from ‘EH’ or ‘OH’ (as in ‘so’) to other vowels in Edge, namely ‘I’ (as in ‘sit’), ‘A’ (as in ‘and’), ‘EH’ and ‘OE’.Once you are comfortable with changing between the modes on different vowels, try it with lyrics.




Sing a phrase that contains both Overdrive and Edge and repeat the exercise, beginning half a note higher each time.Be careful not to sing in too high a pitch for Overdrive.It is better to direct the mode towards Edge sooner rather than later to avoid straining the voice.

### 案例故事 Case Story
A Blues singer with a very large voice started to have problems singing powerfully.It turned out he had been singing with a trio instead of larger bands and with the smaller set-up, the volume of his Overdrive and Edge was too loud.Therefore, he had changed to Curbing, to make the volume more suitable.However, when he returned to the larger band, he was unable to leave Curbing, as he had become used to having a different selection of vowels than in Overdrive and Edge.If he wanted a more powerful volume, he would have to replace Curbing with Overdrive or Edge, as singing loudly in Curbing was straining his voice.We practised the support work and he got used to applying more physical strength.Then we practised the centre of the modes for Overdrive and Edge and he became accustomed to the powerful volume that comes with these modes.Finally, we practised changing the vowels in the higher pitches to ‘EH’ (as in ‘stay’) and ‘OH’ (as in ‘so’) for Overdrive; and to ‘I’ (as in ‘sit’), ‘EH’, ‘A’ (as in ‘and’), and ‘OE’ (as in ‘herb’) for Edge.When he regained control of Overdrive and Edge, he was able to sing just as powerfully as before.


## Exercise reduced density
– from metal to Neutral
If you sing in medium volume and want to stay in reduced density, it is important to be able to get from Curbing, or Overdrive and Edge in reduced density, to Neutral in reduced density - without vocal breaks or changes in the sound.

Sing single notes in Curbing (or Overdrive or Edge in reduced density) on the vowel ‘UH’ (‘EH’ or ‘A’) on A#4 for women or C4 for men.Listen to the reduced density and the slightly plaintive, restrained/contained character and keep this while you repeat the exercise, beginning half a note higher each time.Around D5 for women or D4 for men, gradually decrease the volume and let go of the metallic sound without it feeling uncomfortable.Gradually change the vowel towards ‘AH’.It may help to raise the soft palate a little and keep the twang, or even twang the epiglottic funnel more.Try to approach a softer, more non-metallic, but still restrained/contained character between D5 to E5-F5 for women or D4-G4 for men, without abrupt changes in the sound.You should be in Neutral by E5-F5 for women or G4 for men and can continue singing higher from there.



> Exercise from metal to Neutral in reduced density





If you find it difficult to move from Curbing (or Edge in reduced density) to Neutral without an abrupt change, try the above exercise in reverse.Sing single notes in Neutral from E5-F5 for women or G4 for men and descend by semitones, while trying to make each note a little louder and the sound more restrained/contained.Be careful not to make the note TOO loud as this may result in a change to Overdrive or Edge in fuller density, which might feel uncomfortable and could strain the voice if you maintain the reduced density.If you started in Neutral and made each note a bit louder and more metallic than the previous one, you should be in Curbing (or Edge in reduced density) around C5 for women or C4 for men.

If you experienced a break during the exercise, you started out in Neutral in falsetto.To avoid the break, you can start in Neutral in reduced or fuller density.



> See ‘Remove and avoid unwanted falsetto’


Another possibility is to make a smooth transition from Neutral in falsetto to any other mode or density.



> See ‘Transition to and from falsetto’


Once you can control this transition from Neutral to metal, sing longer sequences, such as scales up and down through three notes, five notes and later through octaves.At first, practise on ‘O’ (as in ‘woman’) as this vowel is usually the easiest in Curbing.Later, practise on other Curbing vowels.For Edge in reduced density, remember to use the contained vowel sounds.Once you are comfortable with changing between the modes on different vowels, try it again with lyrics.

Sing a phrase that contains both Curbing (or Edge in reduced density) and Neutral and repeat the exercise, beginning half a note higher each time.Direct the mode towards Neutral sooner rather than later to avoid straining the voice.

Also practise the ‘transitions using fuller density modes’ and the ‘transition using Curbing, or Overdrive and Edge in reduced density’ described in the chapter ‘Transitions between modes’.



> See ‘Transitions using fuller density modes - Highway’



## Exercise in changing density
If you sing in medium volume and want to change between fuller and reduced density, it is important to be able to get from Curbing or Overdrive and Edge in reduced density, to Edge (or Overdrive) in fuller density, without vocal breaks or changes in the sound.

### From Curbing to Edge
Sing single notes in Curbing on the vowel ‘UH’ (as in ‘hungry’).Start on A#4 for women or C4 for men.Sing in a medium loud volume without it feeling uncomfortable or strenuous.Listen to the reduced density and the slightly plaintive, restrained character and keep this while you repeat the exercise, beginning half a note higher each time.At around D5 for women or E4 for men, gradually change the vowel to ‘A’ (as in ‘And’) and twang the epiglottic funnel.It might also help to raise the larynx, lower the soft palate, broaden the tongue and increase the volume without it feeling uncomfortable.Try to approach the fuller density, and clear and sharp character of Edge between D5 to E5-F5 for women or F4-G4 for men without abrupt changes in the sound.You should be in Edge by E5-F5 for women or F4-G4 for men and can continue singing higher from there.




If you find it difficult to change from Curbing to Edge without an abrupt change, try the above exercise in reverse.。However, be careful not to make the note TOO quiet or lose the metallic character entirely as this might result in the voice changing to Neutral, which may feel uncomfortable and strained.If you started in Edge and made each note a bit quieter and restrained than the previous one, you should be in Curbing around C5 for women or D4 for men.

Once you can control this transition, sing longer sequences, such as scales up and down through three notes, five notes and later through octaves.At first, practise on ‘UH’ and ’A’ (‘Hungry’ and ‘And’) as these vowels are close to both Curbing and Edge.Later, practise changing to the Edge vowel ‘I’ (as in ‘sit’).Then, practise the transition to other vowels in Curbing and Edge, for example changing from Curbing vowels ‘OO’ (as in ‘you’) or ‘O’ (as in ‘woman’), to the Edge vowel ‘OE’ (as in ‘herb’).Once you are comfortable with the transition between the modes on different vowels, try it again with lyrics.

Sing a phrase that contains both Curbing and Edge and repeat the phrase beginning half a note higher each time.It is better to direct the mode towards Edge sooner rather than later to avoid straining the voice.

### From Edge in reduced density to Edge in fuller density
If you sing in medium volumes and want to change between fuller and reduced density, it could also be practical to get from Overdrive and Edge in reduced density, to Edge or Overdrive in fuller density, without vocal breaks or changes in the sound.

Sing single notes in Edge in reduced density on the contained version of the vowel sound ‘A’ (as in ‘And’).Start on A#4 for women or C4 for men.Sing in a medium loud volume without it feeling uncomfortable or strenuous.Listen to the reduced density and the contained character and keep this while you repeat the exercise, beginning half a note higher each time.At around D5 for women or E4 for men, gradually make the vowel sound clearer, still on ‘A’ (as in ‘And’) and twang the epiglottic funnel.It might help to increase the volume, without it feeling uncomfortable.Try to approach the fuller density, and clear and sharp character of Edge between D5 to E5-F5 for women or F4-G4 for men, without abrupt changes in the sound.You should be in Edge in fuller density by E5-F5 for women or F4-G4 for men and can continue singing higher from there.



> Exercise from fuller to reduced density Edge


If you find it difficult to change from Edge in reduced density to Edge in fuller density without an abrupt change, try the above exercise in reverse.Sing single notes in Edge in fuller density from E5-F5 for women or G4 for men and descend by semitones while trying to make each note a little quieter, more restrained and contained.However, be careful not to make the note TOO quiet or loose the metallic character entirely as this might result in the voice changing to Neutral, which may feel uncomfortable and strained.If you started in Edge in fuller density and made each note a bit quieter and contained than the previous one, you should be in Edge in reduced density around C5 for women or D4 for men.

Once you can control this transition, sing longer sequences, such as scales up and down through three notes, five notes and later through octaves.At first, practise on a contained or clear ’A’ (‘Hungry’ and ‘And’).Later, practise the Edge vowel ‘I’ (as in ‘sit’), ‘EH’ (as in ‘stay’) and OE’ (as in ‘herb’).Once you are comfortable with the transition between the modes on different vowels, try it again with lyrics.

Sing a phrase that contains both Edge in reduced density and Edge in fuller density and repeat the phrase beginning half a note higher each time.

This exercise could also be performed in Overdrive, from reduced density to fuller density and vice versa.Just be careful not to sing too high a pitch for Overdrive (especially for women).

### In songs
Sing the song (or lines from a song) changing between densities in medium volume, meaning changing between fuller density and a clear character - and reduced density and a restrained/contained character, in the same volume.Notice that you must change the vowels according to the modes in order to decide which character is being used.Remember to change the vowels in the twanged Edge direction: ‘I’ (as in ‘sit’), ‘EH’ (as in ‘stay’), ‘A’ (as in ‘and’) and ‘OE’ (as in ‘herb’) for Edge; and if you are using Overdrive you need to change to ‘EH’ (as in stay) or ‘OH’ (as in ‘so’).Change the vowels to ‘UH’, ‘O’ and ‘OO’ when singing in Curbing and change to the contained vowels sounds for Overdrive and Edge in reduced density.Be careful not to sing in too high a pitch for Overdrive (especially for women) and remember that the transition must feel comfortable all the way.

Notice, as you get more experienced singing through the modes in songs, how the characters of the modes get less pronounced in the transitions, so that the fuller density with a clear character merges gradually into the reduced density with a restrained/contained character and vice versa in the same volume during transitions.This way, you might end up using many degrees between the fuller and clear character in the highway and the reduced and restrained/contained character in the byway.
