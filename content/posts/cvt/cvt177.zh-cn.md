## 以低密态 “金属” 模式讲话 Speaking in the reduced density metallic modes
Some people speak in the reduced density metallic modes, that means speaking in Curbing, reduced density Overdrive and/or reduced density Edge.The character of the voice here is slightly restrained or contained and the volume is in the medium area.

Because there are 3 different metallic modes in reduced density, you have some possibilities for varying the speech and having a fair range with some tonal variations.

But still, the reduced density has a tendency to become quieter and more difficult to obtain in the lower part of the voice, which is why you should refrain from speaking loudly when speaking in reduced density metallic modes.The reduced density metallic modes do not work in quiet volumes and consequently the voice fails, breaks, splits, creaks or get strained if you hold on this setting in too quiet or too loud a volume.

> ‘Speaking’ example


If people speak in the reduced density metallic modes in a noisy surrounding and feel that it is difficult to be heard, they often try to speak louder and if is too loud for reduced density metallic modes, their voices often tire quickly.It may feel like a volume-limit or as if you have to push your voice out, when trying to speak loudly from this setting.This is a sign that you are pushing the boundaries of the mode, which is something that should be avoided in order not to strain or hurt the voice.To speak healthily in reduced density metallic modes, you must not exceed medium volume at any time.

### Exercises in speaking in reduced Overdrive
At first, practise the Overdrive mode and the reduced density in Overdrive



> See ’Finding Overdrive’


> See ‘Finding reduced density in Overdrive (byway Overdrive)’


If you want to transfer this sound to speech, it must be practised.Start singing a note in Curbing in a comfortable pitch on the vowel ‘UH’ (as in ‘hungry’) in volume 6.Now, change the vowel gradually to ‘EH’ (as in ‘stay’) but be careful that the density does not change.This should be reduced density Overdrive.Notice that the vowel sound becomes a little muffled.If the vowel sound gets too clear and open the note will usually change to fuller density, and you will be singing a highway Overdrive.Now gradually practise maintaining that sound in various pitches from volume 6-7 to 4.To begin with it may be easier to locate the reduced density in a slightly higher pitch that you would with fuller density.

Start in a comfortable pitch and repeat the exercise, beginning half a note lower each time.When you approach the pitch for speaking, try to make a transition from singing to speaking by making the sung note shorter and shorter until you are actually speaking.Make sure that speaking in reduced density Overdrive, just like speaking in the other modes, always feels comfortable.

Now try to practise other vowels.Practise the Overdrive vowels ‘EH’ (as in ‘stay’) and OH (as in ‘so’).Make sure you maintain the reduced density Overdrive character and the medium volume.

Notice the sensation and character when speaking in reduced density Overdrive.Also notice how close you are to the border of fuller density Overdrive.Experiment making the transition from reduced to fuller density, so you at all times can control and know in which density you speak.

This can become handy if you experience inexplicable pain/discomfort when performing the transition from reduced density to fuller density.Then you can either turn the volume up for fuller density or turn the volume down for reduced density.Later you can practise speaking in fuller density in Overdrive in the medium volume, and then perform the transition from reduced density to fuller density in the same volume.This will give you a dynamic and flexible speaking voice, where you do not experience a volume limit or ‘getting stuck’ from only using reduced density.

### Exercises in speaking in 3 reduced density metallic modes
Now practise changing between all vowels in Curbing and in reduced density Overdrive and Edge.Noticed the tiny difference between the restrained sound in Curbing and the contained sound from reduced density Overdrive or Edge.Make sure you can perform free transitions between all the vowels and their reduced modes.If the volume is to exceed medium loud (mf), it is advisable to avoid reduced density metallic vowels and replace them with fuller density Overdrive or Edge vowels.

Practise placing consonants before and after the vowels, but make sure the vowels do not lose their reduced metallic character.Speak loudly and clearly, but make sure it sounds natural.If it is difficult to locate the reduced metallic modes, start by exaggerating the distinctiveness and the volume.Later, when you are able to speak in the reduced metallic modes, make it sound more natural.Practise speaking with short syllables.Later, practise maintaining the energy on several syllables and longer sentences.

Practise just how quiet and how loud you can speak in the reduced density metallic modes on different vowels without loosing the setting.Become familiar with these limits so that you will always be able to move safely within the modes.Take care not to speak too quietly or too loudly as you will lose the reduced density metallic modes and change to either Neutral or fuller metallic modes.

Practise locating several different sound colours in reduced density metallic modes by combining the shape and position of the epiglottic funnel, larynx, tongue, mouth, palate and nasal passage.Locate the lightest and darkest sound colour in your voice and the colours in between.Choose the ones you like and practise speaking with these on both higher and lower notes.

## Speaking almost solely in reduced density metallic modes
Speaking almost solely in reduced density metallic modes can result in a ‘locked-up’ and restrained or contained sound.Examples include people who speak in a restrained/contained and slightly whining manner.The volume largely stays in the medium range, from medium quiet to medium loud volume, If the reduced mode sounds unclear or ‘locked-up’, you might be using too much or too little effort; both may trigger uncontrolled constriction.The range of the speaking voice in reduced density metallic modes may be limited to a certain pitch often in a higher pitch than usual and the speaking voice can be slightly monotonous.The vowels sound similar to each other and the range of volume is around medium.

Take care not to speak too loudly when using reduced density metallic modes, as it can strain the voice and feel uncomfortable.If the volume is to exceed medium loud (mf) it is advisable to avoid reduced density metallic modes and use fuller metallic modes instead.

If you have a sense of ‘being stuck’, practice the transitions to fuller density metallic modes and to Neutral, so you at all times can obtain dynamic changes.

### 案例故事 Case Story
A young, talented blues singer had consulted a doctor for hoarseness.The doctor insensitively told her that her vocal cords were unsuited to singing and that she should give up.She was devastated, but still wanted to find out if there was anything she could do.

Her voice was strained, and she had developed nodules on the vocal cords.Nodules only indicated that the voice needed rest, not that it was unsuited for singing, so I recommended that she be completely silent for 7-10 days.In the meantime, we worked on her support by practising the support without sound.After 10 days of silence, the nodules were gone, and her voice was ready for us to start working on her singing and speaking technique.

It turned out that almost everything she sang was in the fuller metallic modes with a lot of volume, and she found this too loud for speaking.So, she had decreased the volume and subconsciously changed to Curbing.Curbing, however, cannot be used at loud volumes, making it difficult for her when she needed to speak loudly.When she tried, and at the same time used inadequate energy for the support, she became hoarse.At first, we worked on speaking in fuller metallic modes at a powerful volume.When she perfected this, we practised increasing and decreasing the volume in the fuller metallic modes, so she got to know the medium volume in the fuller metallic modes.We also added Neutral for when she wanted to speak quietly.The Neutral mode and the medium volume in the fuller metallic modes also gave her additional possibilities for her singing.Happily, she chose to work with the voice instead of giving up singing.Today she is an outstanding professional singer.
