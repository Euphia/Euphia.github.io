## The vowels’ choice of mode in various volumes
Some vowels choose the mode themselves, according to the volume to make it easier to sing.

Sing each vowel and sing it in different volumes.You will notice that the voice prefers certain modes at certain vowels and volumes.Try to sing all vowels in all modes and in all volumes and you will feel which modes and volume the vowels prefer, to make it easier to sing.The further away you direct the vowel from its easiest place, i.e. its ‘centre’ at a specific volume, the more difficult it becomes, and the more control is required.Which vowels are the easiest to sing in the various modes and volumes varies from singer to singer.




In the above diagram, you can see which modes the vowels choose at various volumes.For example, the vowel ‘I’ (as in ‘sit’) chooses the Neutral mode when sung quietly (p), but it chooses Edge when sung in medium to loud volume (mf-ff).

Remember that the choice of mode can be YOURS, rather than one dictated by the vowels themselves in certain volumes.

### Difficulty of the vowels
In the diagram below you can see which vowels are the easiest and which are the hardest ones in the various modes.In Curbing, for example, ‘OO’ (as in ‘you’), ‘O’ (as in ‘woman’) and ‘UH’ (as in ‘hungry’) are the easiest, whereas ‘EE’ (as in ‘see’), ‘A’ (as in ‘and’) and ‘AH’ (as in ‘far’) are difficult, but will be easier if you direct the vowel towards ‘UH’, ‘O’ and ‘OO’. ‘EH’ (as in ‘stay’) and ‘OH’ (as in ‘so’) are not possible and should be avoided in Curbing




You can also use this diagram to see which vowels can be sung in which modes.For example, the vowel ‘I’ (as in ‘sit’) is easy to sing in Neutral with audible air and in Edge.However, it is difficult to make the vowel in Neutral without audible air, it should be changed towards ‘UH’ for Curbing and ‘I’ should never be attempted in Overdrive.

Remember, how easy a vowel is to sing in the various modes will vary between singers.

### Altering the vowels
In this diagram, you can see the modifications and changes needed for each mode.




If the vowel ‘OO’ (as in ‘you’) is sung in Neutral, for example, there is no need to alter it.In this case, a perfect mode and a precise vowel are attainable.If ‘OO’ is sung in Curbing, it works well too with a restrained character, but if ‘OO’ is attempted in Overdrive, it is necessary to alter the vowel to ‘OH’ (as in ‘so’).If ‘OO’ is attempted in Edge, it must be altered to ‘OE’ (as in ‘herb’) to obtain a perfect mode.

It might seem as all vowels can be used in the low part of the voice because the vocal modes might resemble each other here, but if you want to sing in a specific mode, you need to alter the vowels accordingly.
